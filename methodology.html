Why SubnetROI exists — and how to use it efficiently

Bittensor's network is hard to navigate: 129 subnets, each with its own emission share, alpha price, miner pool, liquidity and risk profile. Most newcomers pick a subnet by word of mouth or a logo, then discover it is unprofitable after paying a registration cost measured in hundreds of TAO.

SubnetROI collapses that into one screen: for your hardware and assumptions, every subnet is ranked by its expected net $/day (revenue minus power, infra, amortized registration and capex). The point is to screen before you commit — a few minutes on this site replaces hours of spreadsheet work, and it updates every 30 minutes from live chain data.

How to use the site efficiently

  1. Set your hardware once — GPU capex + power (or use the tier presets via the API), electricity rate, infra, and your assumed incentive share. These are saved assumptions; the ranking changes with them.
  2. Sort by Net $/d — the default. The top of the list is the best screen for your setup. Check Payback days next to it.
  3. Star subnets (Pro) to build a watchlist that syncs to your key, then hit the ★ filter to see only your candidates.
  4. Model scenarios (Pro): change the alpha price or add locked collateral to see how your net moves before you commit capital.
  5. Watch the registration-cost panel — when the lock cost nears its floor, a registration slot is cheap (see below).
  6. Subscribe to the daily digest — the top-10 subnets by net $/day arrive in your inbox each morning.

What is Bittensor, and why is it so popular?

Bittensor is a decentralized machine-intelligence network: instead of one company (an OpenAI, a Google) training and owning AI models, the network pays anyone to produce useful machine-intelligence work — inference, data, compute, agents, robotics data — and rewards the work that validators judge best. It is orchestrated entirely on-chain.

The network is organized into subnets (~129 live), each a separate marketplace for one type of work:

  • Miner — does the work (serves inference, submits data, runs compute) and is paid in the subnet's alpha token.
  • Validator — checks the work, sets weights, and earns from the subnet's emission.
  • Root / subnet-0 — the network's baseline (no alpha market; not investable — shown as "Root subnet (baseline)" in the table).

Why it is popular

  • Real revenue — the network's top subnets generate meaningful annualized income (Lium ~$8–10M/yr, Targon ~$5.5–6M, Chutes ~$4–5M by the 2026 revenue index).
  • Alpha tokens & dTAO — each subnet's alpha trades with its own price; top subnets have deep liquidity and volatile (opportunistic) markets. In 2026 the first subnets' alphas were listed on a major centralized exchange (Kraken), a first for the ecosystem.
  • Mainstream attention — in September 2026 Grayscale filed with the SEC for a Bittensor ETP, and the network has grown from 32 subnets (2024) to 129, with a 128→256 subnet-capacity expansion underway.
  • Open participation — anyone with a GPU can register and mine; you are paid for demonstrated work, not stake hoarding (though some subnets add collateral locks).

TAO (the network token) is the base currency — registration burns, validator stakes, and most economics are denominated in TAO.

Subnet registrations: lock costs, floors, slots, time-to-floor

Becoming a miner on a subnet means registering a hotkey on-chain — and registration is paid in TAO. There are two entirely different "registration" numbers, and confusing them is the most common beginner mistake:

  • Miner registration (burn) — the cost to join an existing subnet as a miner. It is a dynamic burn: it decays every block and jumps ~26% on each registration (×1.26), with a 360-block half-life. This is the price you actually pay to mine.
  • Subnet creation (lock) — the cost to create a brand-new subnet. This is what the SubnetROI registration panel tracks, and it has its own mechanics below.

Subnet-creation lock costs follow a clean, predictable cycle:

  • Doubling — each new subnet registration doubles the lock cost instantly.
  • Decay — then it decays linearly to a floor over ~100,800 blocks (~2 weeks).
  • Floor estimate — the governance-set minimum (~478 TAO today; it has been lower). The lock cost can never go below this.
  • Rate limit / next slot — only ~one registration per 7,200 blocks (~1 day) network-wide. "Next slot" is when the next registration becomes possible.
  • Time to floor — the time until the decay brings the cost to its floor. The SubnetROI panel fits the post-event decay curve and shows projected cost at 1/3/7 days.

Getting started as a miner (high level)

  1. Scope the subnet first — read its README/whitepaper, check its min_compute.yml, and use SubnetROI to see whether its emission justifies your hardware before you pay anything.
  2. Set up a wallet — Bittensor wallets have a coldkey (your funds/keys, kept secure) and a hotkey (the miner's operational identity). Create both with btcli wallet create. Fund the coldkey with TAO for registration + a little for transaction fees.
  3. Run a node or use an RPC — either run a subtensor node or point btcli at a public RPC endpoint.
  4. Register — btcli subnet register --netuid N (the burn price) or, for a new subnet, btcli subnet create (the lock cost). You receive a UID.
  5. Run the miner — install the subnet's miner code, publish your axon (your serving address), and keep the UID alive. Validators must be able to reach you.
  6. Watch your share — your emissions depend on the incentive share the validators give you. It is positional: top miners capture most, median miners often hover near breakeven. SubnetROI's incentive share input exists to model this reality honestly.
Beginner tip: do not pay the current lock/burn price "because the subnet looks cool." Screener first, register near a cheap registration window, and budget for 2–4 weeks of runtime before judging a subnet. Most subnets are unprofitable for median miners — the top-10 list exists because it is genuinely hard to be in it.

What to look for in a good subnet

Each column in the SubnetROI table is a screen. A healthy candidate checks several boxes:

  • Emission % — the subnet's share of network emissions. Higher is more revenue potential, but also more miners competing. Growth (rising share) beats flat.
  • α price (τ) — the subnet's alpha token value. A high emission with a collapsing alpha price can still lose money; look at miners TAO/day (the actual pool) and your net $/day, not the headline.
  • Volume & liquidity — deep liquidity means you can actually sell the alpha you earn without moving the market. Low-liquidity subnets can have phantom high prices.
  • Chain buys — on-chain alpha purchases per block. Sustained buying is demand for the subnet's token and a floor under price.
  • Dereg risk — flagged subnets risk being deregistered (killed). Avoid unless you know why you're there.
  • Kraken roadmap — subnets whose alphas are listed (or roadmap-listed) on a major CEX: listings historically bring liquidity and price appreciation.
  • Collateral policy — some subnets lock a share of registration as collateral (stake you earn back by mining). Model it with the collateral input so the locked capital doesn't surprise you.
  • Consistency — a subnet that has held its emission share and price for months is safer than a new one with a two-day spike.

The SubnetROI model: formulas, inputs & caveats

The planner ranks all subnets by expected net $/day for a miner running your hardware:

your TAO/day  = miners_tao_per_day × incentive_share
your USD/day  = your TAO/day × TAO price
net $/day     = your USD/day − power − infra − amortized registration − amortized capex
payback       = GPU capex / net $/day

Inputs & what they mean

  • GPU capex / power — what you pay for the hardware and its draw. Power cost = kW × 24h × $/kWh.
  • Infra — monthly hosting, rack, networking (annualized into the daily figure).
  • Incentive share — your assumed fraction of the subnet's miner pool. Positional and competitive: top miners capture most emissions, median miners hover near breakeven. It is an input, not a live measurement.
  • Registration cost — the TAO burned to register a miner, amortized over the period.
  • Alpha price — scenario override: scales every subnet's income by your target α price ÷ its live price. Leave empty to use live prices.
  • Collateral locked — scenario: TAO you lock as miner collateral. The model reports its USD value and an annualized opportunity cost (default 5%/yr) against your net.

Live data — miners_tao_per_day (the subnet's total miner pool), emission %, α price, volume, liquidity and chain buys come from the TaoMarketCap public API, refreshed every 30 minutes; TAO price from CoinGecko. The site falls back to a stored snapshot if the sources are unreachable.

Important caveats — Miner income is alpha-denominated and volatile; emission is not pro-rata to α price (the spec-440 gate redistributes emissions to winners). Collateral figures are scenarios you set — there is no live per-subnet collateral feed. SN 0 is the root subnet (baseline, not investable). Figures are estimates, not predictions — validate against taostats before committing capital.
